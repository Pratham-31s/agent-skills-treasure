# Song Structure

Fallback only. Used when no artist is named, or when no corpus could be retrieved. When a corpus exists, its architecture wins over everything here.

If a corpus exists and its architecture is ambiguous (mixed track structures,
unclear verse/chorus boundaries), fall back to the closest sub-genre default
here — but say so in one line so the user knows the architecture is inferred
rather than measured.

Output stays label-free on every route.

## Genre defaults

Pick the shape that matches the genre, then write without labels.

- **Pop:** 4-line stanzas, 5 stanzas, hook returns twice, 2-line tag to close.
- **Hip-hop / rap:** 8 or 16-line stanzas, 4-line hook, hook after every stanza, ad-lib tails.
- **Folk / country:** 6-line stanzas, narrative arc, hook as a repeated refrain line inside the stanza rather than a block.
- **Rock:** 4-line stanzas, hard 2-line hook, one short bridge that pivots the image.
- **R&B:** 4–6-line stanzas, elongated vowel endings, hook that re-enters mid-stanza.
- **Ballad:** 6–8-line stanzas, no bridge, single repeated closing line.

### Sub-genre detail

When the genre default is too broad for the request, drill in:

- **90s New York boom-bap** (Nas, Biggie, Wu-Tang, Gang Starr, Mobb Deep,
  Rakim, KRS-One): 16-line verses, 4-line hooks, ad-lib tails. Defining trait
  is **dense multisyllabic internal rhyme chains, not just end-rhyme**. Expect
  boroughs, projects, subway, bodegas, 90s-era objects (payphone, cassette,
  boombox). A verse that only end-rhymes is not 90s NY, regardless of topic.
- **Early 2000s Eminem / Shady Records:** 16-line verses, aggressive persona,
  self-referential Slim Shady / Marshall split, internal rhyme chains with
  near-homophone pivots, high profanity. Chorus often staccato or rap-sung.
  Register is unfiltered by default — thinning it is the failure mode.
- **Contemporary melodic rap** (Drake, Post Malone, Juice WRLD): 8–12-line
  verses, half-sung hooks, repetition-heavy, simpler end-rhyme, vulnerability
  markers. Register is conversational, not shouted.
- **Mid-2000s pop-punk / emo:** 4-line stanzas, ABAB, repeated bridge before
  the final chorus, shouted or elongated vowel endings.
- **Nashville country** (Taylor Swift era): 6-line stanzas, narrative arc,
  image → turn → image, hook as a repeated line inside the stanza. Emphasis
  on specific proper nouns and seasonal markers.

## Staging

- Open on an image or an action, never on a thesis.
- One central conceit, reused.
- Turn or reveal at roughly two-thirds.
- End hard: a repeated tag, a cut line, or a spoken tail. No summary stanza.

## When structure is failing

If the draft feels like a form being filled, the conceit is too thin — not the shape. Fix the image before changing the stanza count.

## What not to do

- Do not label. Not once.
- Do not stack a chorus that restates the verse.
- Do not add a bridge to a song under 3 stanzas.
- Do not rhyme-fill a stanza to reach a count.
