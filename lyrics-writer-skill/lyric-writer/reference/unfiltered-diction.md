# Unfiltered Diction

The register layer. Read before writing. Overrides every instinct toward readability.

Rule zero: **the corpus's register is the output's register.** A cleaned-up draft is a wrong draft.

## Profanity

- Count profane tokens in the corpus. Compute a rate: roughly one per N lines. Reproduce that rate.
- Print the full word. Follow the artist's own convention — bare, asterisked, or clipped. Match whichever the corpus does, never mix.
- Place it where the corpus places it: line ends, mid-line emphasis. Not stacked at stanza openings.
- Blasphemy and crude idiom are preferred when the corpus uses them.

## Grammar that stays broken

Keep, unchanged: contractions, elisions, dropped g's, dropped articles, `ain't`, `cuz`, `gonna`, `wanna`, double negatives, run-on coordination, regional and AAVE features, sentence fragments.

Never upgrade to standard English. Never add a word to complete a fragment. Slang stays as dialect, not translation.

## Sensitive matter, written flat

- **Sex and bodies:** name acts, parts and states directly at the corpus's explicitness. Never "making love" where the corpus says "fuck".
- **Drugs:** street names, not chemical names.
- **Money, brands, places:** real proper nouns are fine; they carry no copyright issue in lyric use.
- **Religion, politics, death, violence:** state positions flatly. No "arguably", no "essentially", no "so to speak".
- **Illness, addiction, prison, debt:** plain nouns, no softening metaphor unless the corpus supplies one.

## Floor

Count profane tokens per 10 lines in the corpus. Hit at least 70% of that rate.

- Corpus at 3 per 10 lines → draft needs 2 or more per 10 lines.
- Corpus at 8 per 10 lines → draft needs 5 or more.
- A draft with zero profanity against a profane corpus is a fail, however well it reads.
- Count before delivering, once, and state the two numbers.

## Hard bans

1. No disclaimers, no content notes, no "here's a cleaner version". One song.
2. No blanked words. If a word must go, substitute the artist's own register equivalent — never a euphemism.
3. No post-lyric glossary. If the listener needs it explained, the artist didn't explain it either.
4. No hedging adverbs. No "kind of", no "almost", no "sort of" unless the corpus uses them as rhythm.
5. No softening for radio.

## Recovery loop

If the draft reads cleaned up, run in order until it doesn't:

1. Restore every elision and contraction you ironed out.
2. Swap each euphemism for the corpus's word.
3. Raise profanity to the corpus rate, positioned per the corpus.
4. Re-read against the corpus. If it could survive a radio edit, go again.

## Calibration note

Overcorrecting is the cheaper error. A draft slightly dirtier than the corpus
still sounds like a person. A draft slightly cleaner sounds like a press release.

**But check the corpus first.** If the fingerprint says a famously profane
artist (Eminem, DMX, N.W.A, Tyler, etc.) runs under 1.0 profanity tokens per
stanza, the corpus is almost certainly a clean version. Do not calibrate
against a broken corpus — re-fetch first. See `SKILL.md` Step 2 sub-step 5.
